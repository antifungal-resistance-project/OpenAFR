## Primary target — P. David Rogers / Jeffrey M. Rybak

**Lab:** P. David Rogers group (St. Jude Children's Research Hospital) with Jeffrey M. Rybak
(now University of Tennessee Health Science Center, College of Pharmacy).
**Anchor paper:** Rybak, Sharma, Doorley, Barker, Palmer, Rogers, *"Delineation of the Direct
Contribution of Candida auris ERG11 Mutations to Clinical Triazole Resistance,"* Microbiol
Spectr / AAC 2021, PMID **34878305**. They used Cas9-RNP allele swaps to isolate the direct
MIC contribution of **VF125AL, Y132F, and K143R** in an isogenic *C. auris* background.

**Why they are the best fit for *this* pitch (not just any C. auris lab):**
- They built the isogenic Y132F / K143R strain set. Our pre-registered **Y132F transfer-test
  negative** and the **resistance-retention/size confound** (one-pager §"what's wrong" #2–3)
  are questions *they framed the definitive genetics for* — they are the readers most likely to
  find the honest negative interesting rather than disqualifying (EMAIL.md tier 2).
- They have the exact strains an in-domain-vs-mutant MIC comparison needs, already isogenic —
  which removes the strain-background confound from any wet-lab result.
- Their finding that Y132F/K143R primarily raise *fluconazole/voriconazole* MICs (later
  triazoles minimally changed) is the same asymmetry our confound rests on — a shared mental
  model, so the email lands.

**Personalization sentence:** reference the Cas9-RNP isogenic Y132F/K143R strain set from
PMID 34878305 directly — "your isogenic Y132F/K143R panel is exactly the ground truth my
pre-registered transfer test was trying to predict, and I'd like to show you where it failed."

## Strong alternate — Nathan P. Wiederhold

**Lab:** UT Health San Antonio — **Fungus Testing Laboratory** (a reference susceptibility lab).
**Anchor paper:** Wiederhold et al., *"The Fungal Cyp51-Specific Inhibitor VT-1598 Demonstrates
In Vitro and In Vivo Activity against Candida auris,"* AAC 2018, PMID **30530603** — the exact
VT-1598 evidence behind our T1 confound finding.

**Why they fit:**
- A dedicated reference lab: strains, CLSI M27 plates, and clinical *C. auris* isolates are
  already running (EMAIL.md tier 1). The ten-compound MIC ask is routine there.
- They characterized VT-1598 against *C. auris* — so the "the resistance-active compounds are
  all over my tool's size domain (VT-1598 included)" limitation is one they can immediately
  sanity-check against their own bench data.
- Reference-lab framing also opens the *selectivity* counter-screen (one-pager §"what's wrong"
  #1), which they are equipped to run.

**Personalization sentence:** reference the VT-1598 *C. auris* MIC work (PMID 30530603) — "your
VT-1598 data is the one clean anchor I found for resistance-retained CYP51 activity, and it's
what convinced me my rigid-receptor tool can't honestly claim resistance-breaking."
